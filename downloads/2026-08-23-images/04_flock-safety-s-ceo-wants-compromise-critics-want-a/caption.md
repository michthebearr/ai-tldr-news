💡 Flock Safety's cameras are watching your neighborhood — and almost nobody voted on it.

Flock Safety has quietly become one of the largest private surveillance networks in the United States. Its AI-powered license plate readers and cameras are embedded in communities coast to coast, feeding real-time data to law enforcement. Most residents had no idea until recently.

Now the backlash is arriving. CEO Garrett Langley is pitching "compromise" as the answer — suggesting the company is open to dialogue about guardrails. But critics aren't buying it. They argue that compromise language is a stalling tactic from a company that built a sprawling surveillance infrastructure first and asked for forgiveness later. The oversight mechanisms simply haven't kept pace with the deployment.

This isn't just about Flock. It's a preview of every fight coming over AI in law enforcement — who controls the data, who has recourse when it's misused, and whether private companies should be building public surveillance networks at all.
