💡 AI companies trained their models on your favorite books — and nobody asked the authors.

Writers like George R.R. Martin and John Grisham didn't sign off on their work being fed into AI systems. But whether that counts as copyright infringement? Courts are still figuring it out. The legal hinge point is "fair use" — specifically, whether scraping and training on published books is "transformative" enough to be protected.

The honest answer right now: nobody knows. Lawsuits are stacking up against OpenAI, Meta, and others, but no court has dropped a definitive ruling yet. That legal ambiguity is exactly why AI labs kept building — and why authors kept suing.

Here's what makes this bigger than any one case. Whoever wins sets the blueprint for how every foundation model gets built from here on out. This isn't just a fight over royalties — it's a fight over who owns the raw material of the AI era.
