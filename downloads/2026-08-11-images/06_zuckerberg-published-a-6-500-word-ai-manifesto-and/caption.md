💡 Zuckerberg wrote 6,500 words about AI's future and somehow avoided every question that actually matters.

Mark Zuckerberg dropped a sprawling personal essay this week on "personal superintelligence" and Meta's AI vision. It's heavy on techno-optimism, big swings about the future of humanity, and the kind of language that gets standing ovations at Davos. It is not heavy on privacy, job displacement, or how Meta plans to handle the manipulation risks baked into its own platforms.

TechCrunch's read: this wasn't written for you. It was written for other tech executives — a document designed to position Zuckerberg as a serious AI thinker, not to genuinely reckon with the concerns of the 3 billion people Meta's AI will actually touch.

That's the tell. When a leader produces 6,500 words and still manages to avoid the hardest questions, you're not looking at an oversight. You're looking at a policy.
