💰 Groq raised $350M to buy the exact chips it was supposed to replace.

Once the most hyped name in AI hardware, Groq built its reputation on Language Processing Units — custom silicon designed to make Nvidia irrelevant. Now, the company is pivoting into a "neocloud" business running Nvidia-powered data centers, competing with CoreWeave, Lambda, and every other GPU rental shop fighting for the same customers.

That's not a pivot. That's a surrender.

The $3.5B valuation softens the optics, but the story here is what this says about AI hardware at large. If the company explicitly built to dethrone Nvidia ends up buying Nvidia chips to stay alive, that's the clearest signal yet of how brutal the moat around Jensen Huang's empire really is.

Building a better chip is one thing. Getting the world to adopt it is another problem entirely.
