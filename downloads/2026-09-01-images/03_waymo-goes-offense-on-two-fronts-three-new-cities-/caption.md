💡 Waymo just expanded into three new cities — and took a direct shot at Tesla on the way out.

Denver, San Diego, and Tampa are all getting Waymo robotaxis. It's the company's most aggressive geographic push yet, and it signals something important: Waymo isn't waiting around to see how the autonomous vehicle race shakes out. It's trying to own the map before the competition shows up.

The competition, of course, is Tesla. Right before Cybercab's launch, Waymo went on the record saying Tesla's pure end-to-end AI approach isn't safe enough for real autonomy — and that a sensor-fusion model (cameras, lidar, radar together) is the only responsible path. That's not a technical opinion. That's a strategic move.

The message is clear: we're bigger, we're safer, and we were here first.
