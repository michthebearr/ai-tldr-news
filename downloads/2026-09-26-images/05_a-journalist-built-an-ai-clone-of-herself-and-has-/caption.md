💡 A journalist built an AI clone of herself — and immediately wished she hadn't.

A TechCrunch writer trained an interactive digital avatar of her own likeness and voice, designed specifically to discuss venture fraud. It could hold conversations, mirror her opinions, and sound just like her. On paper, a fascinating experiment. In practice, deeply unsettling.

The experience left her genuinely ambivalent — not because the technology failed, but because it worked. When an AI version of you can represent your views, carry your voice, and interact with strangers, the questions stop being theoretical. Who controls it? What happens when someone else builds one of you without asking?

That's the part that should keep everyone up at night. The tech is already commercially available. The consent frameworks, legal guardrails, and social norms to govern it? Barely a rough draft.
