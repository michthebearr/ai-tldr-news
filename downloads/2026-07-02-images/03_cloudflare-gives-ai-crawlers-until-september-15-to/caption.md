💡 Cloudflare just gave AI companies a deadline — pay up or get blocked.

Starting September 15, AI companies must split their crawlers: one lane for search, a separate lane for AI training and agents. If they don't comply, publishers hosted on Cloudflare's network can block them by default. That's not a polite suggestion — that's infrastructure-level enforcement.

Here's why this hits different. Robots.txt has always been the honor system of the web, and AI companies have largely ignored it. Cloudflare sits in front of a massive slice of internet traffic, which means this policy has actual teeth. They're not asking nicely — they're building the tollbooth.

The endgame is clear: force AI companies into licensing deals with publishers. Content isn't free. The bill is coming due.
