🤖 Humanoid robots have a dirty secret — and it's killing the revolution before it starts.

Everyone's obsessed with the flashy demos. Boston Dynamics flips. Tesla Optimus folds laundry. But behind every smooth robot moment is an ugly truth: someone had to spend hundreds of hours doing that task first, on camera, in a warehouse, so the robot could learn from watching.

That's the data crisis no one's talking about. Physical AI can't just scrape the internet like LLMs did with text. Real-world robot training requires human-generated demonstrations — tedious, manual, unglamorous footage of people completing tasks over and over again. AI labs are already outsourcing this grunt work to companies like XDOF, quietly building a new data labeling industry from the ground up.

Whoever solves this pipeline at scale owns the moat. It won't be the lab with the best hardware or the most funding — it'll be the one that figured out how to feed the machine.
