💡 The US power grid is about to get a kill switch for data centers — and the AI boom just hit a wall.

PJM Interconnection, the largest grid operator in the country, is moving toward a policy that would let it cut power to data centers during peak demand. Not as a last resort. As a managed tool to prevent wider blackouts.

This is the direct consequence of the data center construction boom outrunning the grid's actual capacity. Hyperscalers have been racing to build compute at scale — but the power infrastructure underneath all of it hasn't kept up. Grid operators are out of easy options, and now they're reaching for the circuit breaker.

The "infinite scale" narrative just met a very finite physical ceiling.

If the machines that run AI can be switched off by a utility operator, the entire premise of on-demand, always-on compute gets a lot more complicated. This isn't a software problem you can patch. It's concrete, copper wire, and kilowatts.
