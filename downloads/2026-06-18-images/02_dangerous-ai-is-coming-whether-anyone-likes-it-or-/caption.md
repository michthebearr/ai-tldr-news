💡 Dangerous AI is coming — and it doesn't matter if we're ready.

Ars Technica just published the analysis nobody in the safety community wants to sit with: AI models capable of advanced hacking and cyberoffense aren't a future problem. They're an inevitability. The argument is clean and uncomfortable — capability thresholds keep climbing across dozens of competing labs and nation-states, so if one company taps the brakes, another just accelerates through the gap.

Voluntary restraint doesn't stop the trajectory. It just changes who's driving.

This lands the same week Anthropic's Claude model reportedly had a version pulled after U.S. government officials flagged it as a national security risk — a real-world example of exactly the tension Ars is describing. The safety debate has quietly shifted. The question is no longer *whether* dangerous models will exist. It's who controls them, under what conditions, and whether any meaningful oversight can keep up.
