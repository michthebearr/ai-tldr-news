🤖 Robot brains are finally catching up to robot bodies — and the gap closing is a bigger deal than it sounds.

For years, the hardware has been ahead of the software. Robots could move, grip, and navigate — but their AI couldn't generalize. Every new task meant retraining. Every new environment meant failure. The dream of truly useful physical AI kept hitting the same ceiling.

That ceiling is starting to crack. Researchers are calling this the end of robotics' "GPT-2 era" — functional, impressive in demos, but nowhere near general intelligence. A new class of foundation models built specifically for robotics is emerging, designed to give robot brains the same leap that large language models gave text.

The market is already pricing in the upside. Generalist, one of the hottest physical AI startups right now, just hit a $3B valuation after a $200M raise — less than a year after crossing $2B. When warehouses, surgery, and logistics all unlock at once, that number will look cheap.
