🧠 A man asked ChatGPT about fire before allegedly starting one — and prosecutors used the chat logs to help build their case.

Jonathan Rinderknecht is facing arson charges tied to a New Year's Day 2025 wildfire that became one of the deadliest in LA history. Investigators had the usual toolkit: iPhone location data, security footage, physical evidence. But they also had something new — his ChatGPT history, showing fire-related questions and AI-generated images of flames in the days before the blaze.

OpenAI handed the records over. Just like that.

This is a landmark moment for AI legal precedent. Your chatbot conversations aren't private confessionals — they're stored, they're subpoenable, and now there's a real-world case proving companies will comply. Every sensitive question you've ever typed into an AI assistant exists somewhere on a server. That changes the calculus.

Treat your AI like a search engine. Because legally, it basically is one.
