🧠 OpenAI just built a wall around your data — and admitted it's not bulletproof.

ChatGPT's new Lockdown Mode is a direct response to one of AI's ugliest security problems: prompt injection. That's when a malicious piece of text — hidden in a document, a webpage, an email — tricks the model into doing something you never asked it to do. Like leaking your private information.

When Lockdown Mode is on, ChatGPT restricts what it can do with your sensitive data, even if a bad actor's prompt is telling it otherwise. OpenAI is upfront that this doesn't fully solve prompt injection — it's still possible. But they're calling it a meaningful reduction in attack surface, which in security terms is as honest as it gets.

Here's why this is a big deal: no major AI lab has ever shipped a dedicated, user-facing defense for this exact problem. It's the first acknowledgment that prompt injection isn't just a researcher's concern — it's your concern.
