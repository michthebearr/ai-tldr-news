💡 The influencer you've been following for months might not be a real person — and you'd never know.

AI-generated creators have officially graduated from novelty to industry. We're talking synthetic humans with consistent faces, distinct personalities, curated aesthetics, and posting schedules more reliable than any real creator you follow. They're landing brand deals. They're building engaged audiences. And those audiences? Largely clueless.

What makes this moment different isn't that AI influencers exist — it's that detection has quietly stopped being realistic. The early tells are gone. The uncanny valley is getting patched in real time. Even savvy, chronically-online followers are getting fooled.

That's not just a weird internet quirk. It's a structural problem. Influencer marketing runs entirely on the premise that a real human is vouching for something. Pull that thread, and the whole trust model unravels — not just for brand deals, but for how we relate to social media at all.
