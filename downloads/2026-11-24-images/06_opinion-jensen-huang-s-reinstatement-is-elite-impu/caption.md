💡 Jensen Huang resigned in disgrace — and somehow walked right back into the top job anyway.

The Verge isn't mincing words: this wasn't a comeback story. It was a rescue operation. Huang is back at the helm of Nvidia not because the board independently decided he was the right person for the job, but because the sitting US government effectively made it known he was too strategically important to stay gone. Free market accountability? Not for this guy.

The piece frames it as "too big to fail, the sequel" — and that framing lands. When a CEO can exit under a cloud of scandal and return to power because Washington needs him in the AI race, you're not dealing with a functioning accountability structure. You're dealing with protected class status.

If the most powerful figures in AI are government-shielded assets, real oversight in this industry isn't just difficult. It's structurally impossible.
