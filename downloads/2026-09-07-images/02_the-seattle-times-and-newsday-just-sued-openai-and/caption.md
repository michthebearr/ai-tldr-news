🧠 Two more newsrooms just sued OpenAI and Microsoft — and the timing couldn't be more pointed.

The Seattle Times and Newsday have filed copyright infringement suits against OpenAI and Microsoft, joining a growing wave of media litigation aimed squarely at the AI industry. The suits claim what publishers have been arguing for months: that their archives were vacuumed up for model training without permission or compensation.

The timing stings. OpenAI dropped GPT-6 Astra — its most powerful model yet — right as the lawsuits landed. Regional outlets watching their ad revenue collapse while their journalism quietly powers someone else's product have clearly hit a breaking point.

This isn't noise anymore. It's a drumbeat building toward a legal reckoning that could redefine how AI companies are allowed to train on copyrighted content — and who gets paid when they do.
