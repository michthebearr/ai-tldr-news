💡 Kyle Shanahan said the crash was his fault — now he's saying Autopilot was driving.

The 49ers head coach was involved in a Palo Alto accident earlier this year. His initial statement took personal responsibility. But he's now clarified that Tesla's Autopilot system was engaged at the time of the crash — which changes the story significantly.

That's not a small detail. "Autopilot was on" and "I was driving" are two very different things, and the gap between them is exactly where Tesla's biggest liability questions live. The company markets Autopilot as a driver assistance tool, but study after study shows drivers treat it like full self-driving.

High-profile incidents keep stacking up. And every one of them puts pressure on Tesla to answer a question it's been dodging for years: if drivers genuinely believe the car is in control, whose fault is that?
