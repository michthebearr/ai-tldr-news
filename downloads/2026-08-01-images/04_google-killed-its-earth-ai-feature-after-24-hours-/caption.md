💡 Google built a misinformation machine, shipped it to the public, and took 24 hours to notice.

On Thursday, Google launched a tool that let users generate AI imagery and drop it directly onto real Google Earth satellite maps using text prompts. Sounds cool in a demo. In practice, it was a disaster waiting to happen.

Within hours, researchers had already fabricated refugee camps, fake natural disasters, and invented geopolitical events — all pinned to real locations on real maps. The geographic grounding made the fake imagery feel credible in a way that generic AI art never could. It wasn't just misinformation. It was misinformation with coordinates.

Google pulled the feature Friday. Not because internal review caught it — because the internet did.
