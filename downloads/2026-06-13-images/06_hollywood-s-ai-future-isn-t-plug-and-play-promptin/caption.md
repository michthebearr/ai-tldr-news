💡 The filmmakers actually using AI aren't prompting into the void — they're building custom models trained on their own visual DNA.

At Tribeca 2026, a project called *Dear Upstairs Neighbors* quietly showed what serious AI filmmaking actually looks like. Not Runway. Not Sora. Not a single "generate" button. Instead, the team trained custom versions of Google's Veo and Imagen on their own concept art, color palettes, and visual language — essentially teaching the model to think like them before it made a single frame.

That's a fundamentally different workflow than what's being hyped. The "prompt and hope" pipeline makes for good demos. It doesn't make for films audiences will pay to watch. The gap between those two things is exactly where craft lives — and craft didn't disappear with AI, it just moved upstream.

The real insight here: generic video generators are to serious filmmakers what stock photos are to serious directors of photography. The teams who figure out custom-trained models built around a specific creative vision are the ones who'll actually matter.
