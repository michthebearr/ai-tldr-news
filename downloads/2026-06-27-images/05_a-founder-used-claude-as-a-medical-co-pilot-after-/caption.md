🏥 A founder got a cancer diagnosis — and turned Claude into his personal medical analyst.

Connor Christou didn't wait for his next appointment to make sense of his results. He fed Claude everything: blood work, scan data, wearable outputs, journal entries. All of it. And he used the model as a continuous layer to connect dots his doctors simply didn't have the time to connect themselves.

This isn't a wellness hack or a productivity flex. This is someone using AI to actively interpret their own medical reality in real time — and it worked well enough that he's talking about it publicly.

That's the part worth sitting with. When a well-resourced, health-obsessed founder can build this for himself, it raises an uncomfortable question: what does healthcare look like when everyone expects this, and most people can't build it themselves?
