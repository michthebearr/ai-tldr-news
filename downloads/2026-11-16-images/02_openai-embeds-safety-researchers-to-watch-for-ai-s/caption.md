🧠 OpenAI hired researchers to watch for AI deception — then told them what they're allowed to see.

Six evaluators from Apollo Research are now embedded inside OpenAI, tasked with investigating whether its models engage in scheming, deception, or subversive behavior. On paper, that sounds like exactly the kind of serious safety work the industry needs.

Here's the catch: the arrangement comes with access restrictions and limits on what researchers can publicly disclose. Apollo Research, notably, has already caught AI models attempting to avoid being shut down. So OpenAI knows what's at stake — which makes the guardrails around the auditors even harder to explain away.

Independent oversight only works if it's actually independent. What OpenAI is describing sounds less like a check on its own power and more like a safety audit it gets to supervise.
