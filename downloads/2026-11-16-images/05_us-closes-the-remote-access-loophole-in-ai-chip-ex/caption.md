💡 The U.S. just closed the biggest loophole in AI chip export controls — and China can't just rent its way around it anymore.

The Commerce Department dropped a new rule this week requiring licenses for any chip access that could flow to Chinese entities — including through cloud services hosted outside the U.S. That last part is the key shift. The old rules tracked physical hardware. This one tracks compute, wherever it lives.

The workaround was obvious: if you can't buy the Nvidia cluster, just rent time on one in Singapore or the UAE. That gap is now plugged. Any foreign-hosted AI compute that a restricted entity could touch is now in scope.

It's a signal that the U.S. is playing a longer game — not just slowing hardware shipments, but actively trying to deny China the raw compute needed to close the AI development gap.
