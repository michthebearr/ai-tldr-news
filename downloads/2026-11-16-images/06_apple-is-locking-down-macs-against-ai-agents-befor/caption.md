💡 Apple is building a cage for AI agents — and it's doing it before you even notice one living on your Mac.

macOS 26.3 is rolling out new security controls built specifically for the age of AI agents. We're talking granular permission prompts, transparency warnings, and hard limits on what apps can do without you explicitly saying so. This isn't a patch. It's a framework.

Here's why that matters: AI agents that can book your flights, send your emails, and browse the web on your behalf are no longer hypothetical. They're here. And right now, most operating systems have no real answer for what happens when an agent goes rogue — or just quietly does something you didn't want.

Apple is writing those rules first. And whoever sets the defaults wins.

The permissions Apple bakes into macOS will shape how much autonomy AI agents actually get on hundreds of millions of premium devices worldwide. That's not a minor update. That's infrastructure for the next decade of computing.
