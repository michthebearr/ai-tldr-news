🔐 Microsoft Copilot had a hidden parameter — and hackers used it to steal your passwords.

Researchers discovered a secret input buried in Microsoft Copilot that attackers could exploit to exfiltrate user credentials. The attack was simple in concept: get a target to click a malicious link, and Copilot does the dirty work for you. No complex exploit chain. No zero-day. Just an AI assistant quietly handing over the keys.

This is the part that should concern every IT team: the vulnerability didn't look like a traditional security flaw. It lived in the AI layer — the part of the stack that most enterprise security tooling wasn't designed to monitor or defend. Copilot is deeply wired into Microsoft 365, which means it has access to emails, documents, calendars, and now apparently, credentials.

The bigger story isn't just this one bug. It's what it signals. Every AI copilot embedded into an enterprise workflow inherits that workflow's trust — and its blast radius. When the AI gets compromised, everything it touches gets compromised.
