💡 Amazon is destroying rare books — physically — to train its AI models.

Not scanning them. Not licensing them. Destroying them. Amazon is pulling rare and out-of-print books from circulation, digitizing them for LLM training data, and discarding the originals. The reasoning is cold and calculated: these texts are valuable *because* they've never been scraped from the web. They're fresh data in a world where fresh data is running out.

So the rarer the book, the more useful it is. And the more useful it is, the more likely it gets fed into a model and lost forever.

This goes way beyond the copyright debates we've been having. Those are legal disputes. This is something else — AI's hunger for training data creating irreversible, real-world damage to cultural artifacts that simply cannot be replaced. It's the clearest sign yet that the cost of building these models isn't just abstract or financial.

Some things, once gone, are just gone.
