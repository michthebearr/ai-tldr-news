📱 Meta built a deepfake tool into Instagram without asking you — then acted surprised when people were furious.

The feature let anyone generate AI images using content from public accounts just by tagging them. No consent. No opt-in. Just your face, your photos, your public presence — handed to a generative AI tool by default.

Meta pulled it fast and called it a miss. But "missed the mark" is doing a lot of heavy lifting here. This wasn't a bug. Someone approved this, scoped it, shipped it, and figured the backlash would be manageable.

It wasn't.

The deeper issue: this exposes exactly how blurry the line is between "public content" and "content anyone can use for anything." Posting publicly doesn't mean consenting to AI-generated versions of yourself. Meta just learned that lesson the loud way — and every other platform is watching.
