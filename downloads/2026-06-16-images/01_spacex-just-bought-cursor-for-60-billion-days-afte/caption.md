💰 SpaceX just bought a code editor for $60 billion — and the timing is *very* convenient.

Days after going public, SpaceX has agreed to acquire Cursor, the AI coding assistant, in an all-stock deal worth $60 billion. That makes it one of the largest AI acquisitions in history. From a rocket company.

Here's the play: SpaceX sold IPO investors on a $26 trillion AI addressable market. Now they need something to back that up. Cursor gives them an actual AI software product — fast. And using freshly printed post-IPO stock as currency means they're technically spending money they just raised without spending money they actually have.

Bold move. Convenient math.

What this really signals is how panicked the race for AI software dominance has gotten. A company whose core business is putting things into orbit just paid $60 billion for a tool that helps developers write code faster. Every major player wants to own an AI wedge — even when it has nothing to do with what they actually do.
