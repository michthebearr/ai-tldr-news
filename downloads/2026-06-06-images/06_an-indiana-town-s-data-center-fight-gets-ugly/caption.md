💡 A mayor in Indiana just said the quiet part loud — and it's blowing up in his face.

Shelbyville, Indiana is fighting over a proposed $2 billion data center. Residents are pushing back over power demands, water usage, noise, and neighborhood disruption. Pretty standard stuff for a community asked to host the physical backbone of the AI boom.

Then Mayor Scott Furgeson got caught on camera saying "No Data Center" signs only show up in "shitty houses" — and that "most of them are rentals." The clip went wide instantly. Because nothing unites a protest movement faster than a politician telling residents their opinions don't count because of their zip code.

This isn't just a local story. Data center siting is becoming one of the hottest political flashpoints in America. Communities are being asked to absorb real costs — to their infrastructure, environment, and daily lives — in exchange for economic promises that don't always materialize. When officials respond with contempt instead of answers, opposition movements don't shrink. They organize.
