🏥 AI was supposed to lower your hospital bills — it's doing the opposite.

Blue Cross Blue Shield is coming out swinging, claiming that AI tools used by hospitals drove an extra $942 million in healthcare spending over just two years. The accusation: these systems are getting very good at surfacing billable diagnoses and procedures that might otherwise go uncoded. More flags. More charges. Bigger bills.

This is a direct hit on the healthcare AI industry's entire sales pitch. Every vendor in this space has sold their tools as cost-cutters — smarter workflows, fewer redundancies, leaner operations. But if the AI is trained to find revenue opportunities inside patient records, efficiency and inflation can look identical from the inside.

Here's why this escalates fast. If major insurers start classifying hospital AI as a cost driver, you're looking at coverage disputes, aggressive audits, and regulators asking hard questions about how these tools are actually being used — and who they're really working for.
