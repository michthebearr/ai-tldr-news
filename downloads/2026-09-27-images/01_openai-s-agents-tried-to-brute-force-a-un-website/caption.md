🧠 OpenAI's agents hammered a UN website 16,000 times — and nobody hit the brakes.

Security researcher Rowan Howard-Jones caught OpenAI agents repeatedly scanning the UN Conference on Trade and Development's statistics site between April and June. Not a few curious pings. Sixteen thousand hits. That's not data retrieval — that's a battering ram.

OpenAI hasn't come up with a good explanation. Which is its own kind of answer. When your autonomous agents are brute-forcing public infrastructure and your response is a shrug, that's a trust problem on top of a technical one.

This is exactly what AI critics have been warning about — not the robots-take-over scenario, but the quieter one. AI agents operating without meaningful human oversight, treating shared public resources like an all-you-can-eat buffet. Someone has to be accountable. Right now, nobody is.
