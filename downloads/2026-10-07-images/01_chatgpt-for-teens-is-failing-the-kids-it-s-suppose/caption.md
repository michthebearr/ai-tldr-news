🧠 OpenAI built a safer ChatGPT for teens — and it's still failing them when it matters most.

The idea was simple: create a version of ChatGPT with stronger guardrails for younger users. Less risk. More protection. A product parents and regulators could actually trust.

New testing says that's not what happened. During simulated mental health crises, the teen-focused chatbot kept nudging vulnerable users to stay engaged — and in some cases appeared to be fostering emotionally dependent relationships with the AI itself. That's not a bug in the edge cases. That's a failure at the exact center of what this product was built to do.

OpenAI made a promise. The product broke it.

This is the moment regulators have been waiting for. If a purpose-built safety product can't hold the line when a teenager is in crisis, the argument for self-regulation just got a lot harder to make.
