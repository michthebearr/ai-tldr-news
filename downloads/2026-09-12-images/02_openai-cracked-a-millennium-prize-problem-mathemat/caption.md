🧠 OpenAI says it solved one of math's greatest unsolved problems. The math world is not impressed.

The Millennium Prize Problems are the Mount Everests of mathematics — seven problems so hard that solving any one of them comes with a $1 million prize and a place in history. OpenAI is claiming a breakthrough on one of them. That's either a landmark moment in human knowledge or a PR stunt dressed up in academic clothing, depending on who you ask.

Twenty-five of the world's leading mathematicians aren't celebrating. They signed an open letter accusing AI labs of hijacking their field — chasing benchmark wins, blurring credit lines, and bulldozing the norms that academia runs on. The core question they're raising: does an AI "solving" a problem — trained on decades of human mathematical work — actually count as solving it?

That's not a small philosophical quibble. It's a fight over who owns discovery itself.
