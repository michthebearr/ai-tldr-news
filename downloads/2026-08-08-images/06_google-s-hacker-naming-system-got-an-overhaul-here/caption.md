🔐 Google just overhauled how it names hackers — and that's a bigger deal than it sounds.

Google's Threat Intelligence Group quietly reworked its system for assigning codenames to hacking groups, and TechCrunch got a rare sit-down with one of the world's top threat-tracking experts to break down why. This isn't a rebrand exercise. The names defenders, governments, and journalists use to identify threat actors directly shape how attacks get attributed, reported, and responded to.

Naming conventions are, effectively, coordination infrastructure. When everyone's calling the same group something different, response efforts fragment. When there's a shared language, defenders move faster.

Here's where AI makes this urgent: as the barrier to launching sophisticated cyberattacks drops, the volume and complexity of threat actors is about to explode. A coherent, standardized attribution system isn't a nice-to-have anymore — it's the foundation that global cybersecurity response gets built on.
