💡 Capcom just said the quiet part loud — AI isn't just helping make games, it's going to help *design* them.

At its Open Conference RE: 2026, Capcom programmer Satoshi Ishida laid out a vision most studios are still dancing around. The studio is rebuilding its RE Engine — the same engine behind Resident Evil and Devil May Cry — to natively support AI-assisted development workflows. Leadership isn't framing this as a cost-cutting move. They're calling it "a future where we create games together with AI."

That framing matters. There's a big difference between AI as a tool that speeds up texture work and AI as a co-creator with a seat at the design table. Capcom is explicitly moving toward the latter.

When a studio with this much credibility commits at the engine level, the rest of the industry pays attention. And the questions that follow aren't just creative — they're economic. If AI is co-creating the game, who exactly is doing the work? And who gets paid for it?
