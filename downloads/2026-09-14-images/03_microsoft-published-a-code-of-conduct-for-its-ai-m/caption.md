💡 Microsoft just told its AI models how to behave — in writing.

The company released a formal code of conduct for its AI systems this week. No hacking. No deceiving users. Support humans, don't replace them. Oh, and "accelerate human flourishing." You know, just the basics.

On the surface, it reads like a PR document dressed up as a policy. But there's something more calculated happening here. By putting these principles on paper, Microsoft is handing regulators, enterprise clients, and competitors a benchmark — one they can actually be held to. That's either bold accountability or a very polished liability shield, depending on how cynical you are.

Either way, a code of conduct is only as good as the enforcement behind it. The real story isn't the document. It's what happens the first time the models don't follow it.
