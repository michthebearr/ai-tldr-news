💡 Jensen Huang is out — and the AI startup world just lost its most powerful checkbook.

Nvidia under Huang wasn't just building chips. It was quietly functioning as one of the most aggressive capital allocators in tech history — deploying tens of billions into AI startups and propping up valuations across the ecosystem. Part chipmaker, part sovereign wealth fund, full leather jacket.

Now he's gone. And VCs and founders aren't hiding their anxiety. Smaller checks. Compressed valuations. A Nvidia-shaped hole in the funding market that no single player is positioned to fill. The concern isn't abstract — Huang was often the difference between a startup's Series B happening or not.

The timing couldn't be worse. AI infrastructure costs are still brutal, competition is intensifying, and the one guy who seemed willing to bet everywhere, on everything, just left the building.
