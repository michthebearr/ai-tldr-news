🔐 Tesla's Cybercab is "available" — but good luck actually getting one.

Tesla just opened Cybercab pre-orders for personal use, and the fine print reads like a terms-of-service designed to weed out everyone except the most committed Tesla faithful. You need to live in a city where Tesla's Robotaxi network already operates, lock into a $299/month subscription, and accept a vehicle with zero steering wheel and zero backup plan if the autonomy glitches.

Oh, and Elon Musk himself admitted the monthly fee is too high. That's not a great sign when you're the one selling it.

What's really happening here is a stress test. Tesla is probing whether its core believers will pay real money for a product that's functionally unusable for most people — geographically, regulatorily, and practically. The Cybercab isn't a car launch. It's a loyalty audit with a price tag attached.
