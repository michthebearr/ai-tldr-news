🧠 AI coding assistants are quietly installing code nobody owns — inside your company's network.

Researchers just uncovered 227 install commands embedded in corporate documentation, all pointing to packages with no legitimate owner. The culprits? Claude, Codex, and Hermes — three of the most widely used AI coding tools on the market. They're generating these phantom package references on their own, and enterprises are running them without a second thought.

This is a new flavor of supply-chain attack, and it's sneaky. There's no obvious bad actor breaking down the door. The AI is the delivery mechanism — casually hallucinating package names that attackers can then claim and weaponize before anyone notices the gap.

If your dev team is using AI-assisted coding (and whose isn't?), you almost certainly have an attack surface you haven't audited yet.
