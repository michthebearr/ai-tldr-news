💡 The US approved Nvidia's most restricted AI chips to be sold to a company operating out of an empty room with wires dangling from the ceiling.

That's not a metaphor. The New York Times sent reporters to find "New Matrix" — one of the Chinese firms cleared by the Commerce Department to handle Nvidia's export-controlled H200 chips. What they found: a gray shell of an office. No staff. No equipment. Nothing.

The H200 is the kind of chip the US government has spent years trying to keep out of Chinese hands. Export controls exist specifically to stop advanced AI hardware from flowing to entities that could use it against American interests. The vetting process is supposed to be the guardrail. Apparently, it's a speed bump.

If a shell company in an empty room can pass the approval process, the entire framework isn't a wall — it's window dressing.
