🧠 An Anthropic mathematician may have just solved the most famous unsolved problem in math — and he used Claude to do it.

Levent Alpöge, a mathematician at Anthropic, claims he's cracked the Riemann hypothesis. That's one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems — a list of questions so hard that solving any single one comes with a $1 million prize and a permanent place in history. This one has been open for over 160 years.

He didn't do it alone. Alpöge used Claude as a collaborator throughout the proof process. Not as a calculator. Not as a spell-checker. As a genuine thinking partner working through one of the deepest problems in mathematics.

The proof hasn't been independently verified yet — and that's the only thing standing between "interesting claim" and "world-changing moment." Mathematical proofs at this level take time to scrutinize. But if it holds up, this isn't just a win for Alpöge. It's a signal that AI has crossed a threshold most people weren't ready for.

Science just got a new co-author.
