🔐 The "first AI ransomware attack" wasn't what the headlines made it sound like.

Last week, media went wall-to-wall with a terrifying milestone: an AI agent autonomously executed a ransomware attack in the wild. The reality? A human chose the target, built the infrastructure, and handed over stolen credentials. The AI just handled the technical execution. That's a very different story.

It's still a first. It's still worth paying attention to. But "AI autonomously committed cybercrime" and "AI completed steps a human set up for it" are not the same headline — and that gap matters enormously.

Here's the real danger: when the hype outpaces reality this badly, it cuts both ways. It panics people who don't need to be panicked yet, and it hands actual bad actors a blueprint they might not have considered. Irresponsible framing isn't just sloppy journalism — it has consequences.
