💡 Turns out "unlimited AI access" was a great idea — until the CFO saw the bill.

Companies that handed employees open-ended AI subscriptions are now doing the math, and the numbers are ugly. We're talking $30,000 a year per person, burned on things like summarizing a calendar or rewriting a Slack message five different ways. The tokenmaxxing era had a good run. It's over.

Rippling CEO Parker Conrad is among the execs surfacing the uncomfortable truth: AI ROI is wildly uneven across workforces. A handful of power users are generating real value. A much larger chunk is essentially running an expensive autocomplete habit on the company card.

The response is a new layer of enterprise bureaucracy — usage caps, approval workflows, spend dashboards. And quietly, a whole new software category is emerging just to manage AI costs. The productivity revolution is real. So is the audit.
