💡 Uber burned through its entire annual AI budget in four months. Walmart quietly throttled its AI coding tool. The free-for-all is over.

Both companies made the same bet: tell employees to use AI freely, watch productivity soar. What they didn't plan for was the bill.

Uber actively encouraged staff to go all-in on AI tools — then hit its annual spending cap before summer. Walmart's internal coding assistant, Code Puppy, got quietly throttled after LLM costs blew past projections. These aren't scrappy startups miscalculating. These are two of the largest enterprises on the planet.

"Use AI as much as you want" was never a strategy. It was a vibe.

The unbounded-access era of corporate AI is ending fast. The next phase looks a lot more like rationing, usage tiers, and hard caps — which raises a real question: if you're telling employees to transform how they work with AI, but limiting how much AI they can actually use, what exactly are you asking them to do?
