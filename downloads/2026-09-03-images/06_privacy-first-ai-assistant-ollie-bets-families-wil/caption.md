💡 A new AI assistant wants to know everything about your family — and promises to forget all of it.

Ollie is entering the AI assistant space with a pitch that sounds almost radical in 2025: give us deep access to your life, and we won't use it to train our models or sell it to anyone. Schedules, habits, personal details — all of it stays yours.

That's a direct shot at every major player in the room. Meta is literally paying users for their data. OpenAI is building training sets at scale. The entire business model of consumer AI has quietly assumed your personal information is the price of admission.

Ollie is betting families are ready to pay actual money instead.

The catch is always credibility. Promises are easy; verification is hard. But if Ollie can make the privacy guarantee stick — and make parents believe it — there's a real gap here worth filling.

The most valuable AI assistant might not be the smartest one. It might be the one you actually trust.
