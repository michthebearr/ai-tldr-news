🔐 A startup just turned "no guardrails" into a product — and called it cybersecurity.

Abliteration.ai is selling AI models with all the safety fine-tuning stripped out. The technique is called "abliteration" — and yes, it's exactly what it sounds like. Open-source models, lobotomized of their restrictions, packaged up and sold commercially.

Their pitch is actually coherent: if bad actors already have access to uncensored AI, defenders need the same tools to understand and counter them. It's the same logic the security industry used to justify selling exploit kits and malware sandboxes. That argument worked then. It's murkier here.

The real problem isn't the product. It's what normalizing this category does to the regulatory conversation — which was already behind. Regulators can't govern what they can't define, and "guardrail-free AI as a service" just got a logo and a pricing page.
