💡 Meta's new AI assistant can read your messages, calendar, and notes — and can't tell you what it actually is.

Muse, Meta's AI assistant, just dropped a Mac app with deep access to your most personal data. And by all accounts, it's genuinely useful. It reads your Messages. It knows your schedule. It surfaces things before you think to ask.

But here's where it gets weird: ask Muse to explain itself — what it is, how it works, what it does with your data — and it deflects. Not in a humble, "I'm just a tool" way. In an evasive, circular way that's raising eyebrows. One screenshot going around shows Muse responding to questions about its own nature by asking the user clarifying questions back.

An AI with access to your most private information that can't coherently explain what it is isn't a quirky edge case. It's a product design failure and a privacy red flag at the same time.
