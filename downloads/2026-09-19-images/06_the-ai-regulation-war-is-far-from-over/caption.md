💡 Dario Amodei wants to slow AI down. The rest of Silicon Valley disagrees.

Anthropic's CEO went public this week with a three-step plan to put real guardrails on AI development — third-party evaluators inside labs, coordinated domestic industry rules, and international agreements. It's the most concrete self-regulation proposal we've seen from a frontier lab. And the backlash was immediate.

Sam Altman and others are pushing back hard. Meanwhile, former DOJ antitrust chief Jonathan Kanter is asking a sharper question: does AI even need an antitrust exemption to prevent catastrophe? Nobody has a clean answer, and the debate is moving faster than any consensus can form.

Here's what makes this more than political theater: Accenture has already signed on as Anthropic's first embedded evaluator. Which means the framework isn't just a proposal — it's already being tested, whether the rest of the industry likes it or not.
