🧠 OpenAI and Microsoft called their own data scraping "the largest theft of labor in human history" — in their own documents.

Newly unsealed court docs from the New York Times' lawsuit reveal something extraordinary: the companies weren't just aware their training data practices were controversial — they internally described them as creating a "doom loop" that would damage the web, and admitted their scraping made a "complete mockery of the idea of fair use."

That's not a critic talking. That's them.

This matters because fair use has been the entire legal foundation AI companies have leaned on to justify training on copyrighted data. If their own documentation undermines that defense, the NYT's case just got a lot stronger — and the ripple effects across the industry could be massive.

Every AI lab building on scraped data should be paying very close attention right now.
