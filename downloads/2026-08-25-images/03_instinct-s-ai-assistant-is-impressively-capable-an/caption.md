💡 Instinct's new AI assistant can do almost anything for you — and that's exactly the problem.

Early testers are genuinely impressed. Instinct is capable, fast, and feels like the AI assistant people have been waiting for. It can take actions on your behalf, navigate apps, and get things done in ways most assistants still can't.

But here's the catch: to do all of that, it needs deep system access. Broad terms of service. Permission to act in your name. Privacy and security researchers are already raising flags — because the same capabilities that make it powerful are the exact ones that make it dangerous if something breaks, gets hacked, or gets misused.

The more an AI can do for you, the more it can do *to* you.

This is the defining tension of the AI agent era. Instinct is essentially a live experiment in how much access users are willing to hand over — and whether the industry has any real answer for where "useful" ends and "reckless" begins.
