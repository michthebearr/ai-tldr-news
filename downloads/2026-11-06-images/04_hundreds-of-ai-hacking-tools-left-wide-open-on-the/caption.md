🔐 Hundreds of AI-powered hacking tools are sitting on the open internet — no password required.

Security researchers found hundreds of exposed servers running Artex, an AI-powered penetration testing tool, with zero access controls. No login. No credentials. Just a fully loaded AI hacking suite, free to whoever stumbles across it.

That's not just a misconfiguration. That's a loaded weapon left on a park bench.

The bigger issue isn't sloppy IT hygiene — it's what happens when offensive AI tooling gets handed to the wrong people at scale. These aren't passive data leaks. They're force multipliers. Someone with bad intentions and no technical background can now punch way above their weight class, courtesy of an unsecured server someone forgot to lock.

As AI security tools get more powerful, the blast radius of leaving them exposed gets dramatically larger. This is a genuinely new threat surface — and right now, the industry isn't taking it seriously enough.
