🧠 Two more news outlets just sued OpenAI and Microsoft — and the legal walls are closing in.

The Seattle Times and Newsday have filed suit against OpenAI and Microsoft, claiming their journalism was scraped without permission to train commercial AI models. They're not alone. The New York Times, and a growing list of publishers, are making the exact same argument: this isn't fair use, it's theft.

The core question is one the AI industry has been dodging since day one — can you build a billion-dollar product on content you never paid for? Courts are now being asked to answer it formally, and the case count is starting to look less like scattered noise and more like a coordinated reckoning.

A ruling against OpenAI here wouldn't just cost money. It could force a fundamental restructuring of how frontier AI models are trained on web data. That's existential territory.
