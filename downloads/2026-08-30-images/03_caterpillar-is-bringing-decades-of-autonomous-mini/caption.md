🤖 Caterpillar has been running autonomous machines in the middle of nowhere for 30 years — and enterprise AI is finally paying attention.

While tech companies were still debating AI deployment frameworks, Caterpillar was already running fully autonomous haul trucks in remote mines with zero IT support, extreme temperatures, and zero tolerance for failure. A software glitch didn't mean a bad user experience. It meant a $5 million machine sitting dead in a pit.

That kind of pressure builds a different kind of discipline. Now Caterpillar is applying those same principles — rigorous edge case planning, ironclad reliability standards, real-world failure modeling — to its broader AI rollout strategy.

Enterprise AI has a maturity problem. Most organizations are deploying AI with the confidence of someone who's never had one break at the worst possible moment. Caterpillar's industrial-grade playbook might be exactly the model they need.
