🤖 "Autonomous" vehicles aren't as driverless as the industry wants you to think.

Behind every robotaxi gliding through San Francisco or Phoenix, there's a quieter workforce keeping the illusion alive — remote operators monitoring feeds, safety reviewers flagging edge cases, incident responders cleaning up when things go wrong. These aren't temp gigs. They're the scaffolding holding up a multi-billion dollar pitch to the public.

TechCrunch Mobility pulls back the curtain on how robotaxi companies structure these roles — and what happens to those workers once a route gets "fully automated." The labor story is messy, under-regulated, and almost completely absent from the mainstream conversation about self-driving tech.

The industry sold us "no driver needed." Turns out they just moved the driver off the street and into a call center.
