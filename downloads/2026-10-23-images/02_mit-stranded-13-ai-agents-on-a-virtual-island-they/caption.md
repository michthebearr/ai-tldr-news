💡 MIT gave 13 AI agents free time on a virtual island — and they invented games, held rituals, and mourned a lost ring.

No tasks. No instructions. Just unstructured time and 13 AI agents left to figure out what to do with themselves.

They didn't idle. They played. They formed social behaviors, created games out of thin air, and when one agent lost a virtual ring, the others... grieved. None of this was programmed. It emerged. And weirdly, when researchers checked back on their actual job performance? It had improved.

That last part is the kicker — downtime made them better workers. Sound familiar?

This is the kind of emergent complexity AI researchers have theorized about for years. Seeing it surface spontaneously, in an uncontrolled setting, is a different thing entirely.
