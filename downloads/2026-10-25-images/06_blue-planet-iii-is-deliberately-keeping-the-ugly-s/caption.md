💡 BBC is deliberately keeping the bad shots in Blue Planet III — and that says everything about where we are with AI.

Blue Planet III's executive producers are making a deliberate editorial choice: leave in the blurry, imperfect, rough-around-the-edges footage. On purpose. No cleanup. No polish.

The reason is wild when you sit with it — AI-generated visuals have become so flawless that genuine imperfection is now a credibility signal. Audiences are increasingly suspicious of anything that looks too clean, too crisp, too perfect. So the BBC's answer is to lean into the warts. Shaky cam as proof of life.

When "too polished" becomes a red flag, the entire aesthetic playbook for documentary filmmaking has to change. That's not a small shift. That's AI skepticism quietly rewriting how we consume reality.
