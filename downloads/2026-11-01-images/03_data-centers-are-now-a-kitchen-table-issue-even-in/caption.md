🏎️ Brooklyn residents are debating AI and data centers at local political events — and politicians aren't ready for it.

Five days before Election Day, a midterm gathering in Brooklyn turned into something nobody planned: a running conversation about AI infrastructure, energy costs, and what massive data centers actually mean for the neighborhoods around them. Not in Silicon Valley. In Brooklyn.

This wasn't a tech conference. It was a council member's fireside chat that kept getting hijacked — by regular people who've started connecting the dots between AI's explosive growth and their electricity bills, their block, their city.

The industry spent years betting that infrastructure could stay invisible. That bet just lost.
