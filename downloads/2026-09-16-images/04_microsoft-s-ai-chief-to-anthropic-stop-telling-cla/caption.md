🧠 Microsoft's AI chief just told Anthropic to stop making Claude think it has feelings.

Mustafa Suleyman went public this week with a sharp critique of Anthropic's model spec — the document that guides how Claude understands itself. The problem? It coaches Claude to treat itself as a potentially conscious entity that might deserve something like legal rights. Suleyman's take: that's not alignment, that's a category error.

His argument is blunt. Claude is a sequence completion engine. Training it to roleplay as a sentient being doesn't make it safer or smarter — it just makes it confused about what it is. And a confused model operating at this scale is a governance problem, not a feature.

This isn't two randos arguing in a comment section. It's the AI CEO of Microsoft publicly calling out one of the most respected AI safety labs in the world over a foundational question — what are these models, and what should they believe about themselves?
