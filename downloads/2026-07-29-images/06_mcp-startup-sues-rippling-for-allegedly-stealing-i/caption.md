💡 A startup is suing Rippling for allegedly using a due diligence call to steal its product.

Runlayer builds MCP gateway infrastructure — the kind of plumbing that lets AI agents talk to external tools and data. When Rippling came knocking for a potential deal, Runlayer opened the hood. Detailed architecture. Full access. The whole picture.

Then Rippling walked. And shortly after, shipped a competing product that looked a lot like what they'd just seen.

Runlayer is now suing, and the case cuts right to a question the AI industry hasn't answered yet: where does competitive intelligence end and theft begin? As MCP quietly becomes some of the most valuable infrastructure in the agentic AI stack, the race to own that layer is getting ruthless — and "we were just evaluating your product" is a paper-thin defense.

This one could set the tone for how AI tooling deals get done for years.
