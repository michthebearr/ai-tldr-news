💡 The New York Times just sued a browser — and it changes everything about AI's data problem.

The NYT has expanded its AI copyright war beyond the obvious targets. They're now suing Brave, the privacy-focused browser, for allegedly scraping copyrighted content and funneling it to AI companies like Anthropic and Perplexity. Other publishers have joined the suit.

This is a significant strategic shift. Instead of only going after the AI companies training on the data, publishers are now targeting the infrastructure layer — the pipes that collect and distribute the content in the first place. If you can't win by suing the end user, go after the middleman.

And if it works, the consequences are massive. A legal precedent holding browsers liable for content aggregation could shatter the web scraping pipeline that AI training runs on.
