🔐 Nvidia doesn't just want to power AI — it wants to police it.

Jensen Huang unveiled a new platform this week built to put independent security guardrails around AI agents that go rogue. We're talking dedicated software and hardware designed to catch AI systems when they go off-script — before they cause real damage.

And the timing isn't random. "Rogue agent" incidents have quietly become one of the industry's most uncomfortable conversations. The debate is split: is this a deep alignment problem that can't be engineered away, or just a reliability issue that better infrastructure can fix? Nvidia is firmly betting on the latter — and building a business around it.

Here's the bigger play: by positioning itself as the safety layer, not just the compute layer, Nvidia becomes essential no matter which AI lab wins the model race. That's a massive market expansion — and a very smart hedge.
