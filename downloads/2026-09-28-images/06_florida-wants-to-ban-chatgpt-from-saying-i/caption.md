🧠 Florida wants to make it illegal for ChatGPT to say "I."

Florida's Attorney General is asking a court to ban OpenAI from giving ChatGPT what the state calls "false human attributes" — first-person pronouns, emotion-mimicking language, the whole thing. The argument: it tricks users, especially kids, into believing they're talking to something that actually feels and thinks.

This builds on a lawsuit filed earlier this year, and it's more serious than it sounds. If a court agrees that saying "I feel" or "I think" constitutes deception, OpenAI doesn't just get a slap on the wrist — it may have to fundamentally rebuild how ChatGPT communicates with every single user.

The industry-wide implications are massive. Every major AI assistant — Gemini, Claude, Copilot — uses the same human-ish conversational framing. A Florida win here could force a total rethink of how AI is allowed to talk to people.
