🧠 Sam Altman filed IPO paperwork — then told everyone an IPO would be a bad idea.

In a wide-ranging Fortune interview, OpenAI's CEO pumped the brakes on a 2026 public offering, calling it "ill-advised." Which would be a lot more reassuring if OpenAI hadn't already quietly filed confidential IPO paperwork. Make that make sense.

The interview covered a lot of ground. Altman talked recursive self-improvement, weighed in on the Hugging Face hacking incident, and — casually — acknowledged it's "absolutely" possible to build an AI beyond human control. He promised he'd act to prevent that. Cool, cool.

Here's the real tension: OpenAI's IPO timeline isn't just a business decision. It directly shapes how the company balances its capped-profit model against investor pressure — and whether its safety mission survives contact with Wall Street.
