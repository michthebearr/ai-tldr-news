🤖 The robotaxi free-for-all is officially over.

Cities and states are done being test tracks. After a string of high-profile incidents — blocked emergency vehicles, confused cars, public trust in freefall — regulators are moving fast to put real guardrails on autonomous vehicle deployments. The "move fast and break things" playbook doesn't work when the things breaking are city streets.

For years, AV companies operated under a quiet understanding: deploy aggressively, learn publicly, and deal with the consequences later. That window is closing. Lawmakers who once barely understood the technology are now writing legislation that will define what autonomous vehicles can and can't do — and they're not asking the industry for permission first.

Here's the bigger picture: how regulators handle robotaxis in the next 12-18 months won't just affect Waymo or Tesla. It sets the template for how every physical-world AI system gets governed. Drones, robots, automated logistics — all of it. The precedent being written right now is enormous.
