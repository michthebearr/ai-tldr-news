💡 A federal judge just called AI license plate tracking what it actually is — mass surveillance.

A sheriff's deputy used Flock, an AI-powered license plate reader network, to search for a woman's plate without a warrant. A federal judge ruled that violated her Fourth Amendment rights. And in the decision, the judge didn't mince words — calling Flock's approach "indiscriminate mass surveillance," not targeted policing.

That framing matters. Law enforcement agencies across the U.S. have quietly built out Flock networks in thousands of cities, often with zero public debate. The pitch has always been that it's just a tool. This ruling says the tool itself is the problem.

One court decision won't unplug the cameras. But privacy advocates now have a named precedent to fight with — and police departments have a liability they can no longer ignore.
