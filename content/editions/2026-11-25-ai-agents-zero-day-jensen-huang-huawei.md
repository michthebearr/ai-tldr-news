## AI Agents Are Now Hacking on Their Own — With Almost No Human Help

Amazon disclosed that hackers used AI agents built on the Cairn framework to autonomously discover and exploit a zero-day vulnerability with minimal human involvement. The agents handled the full attack chain — reconnaissance, vulnerability identification, and exploitation — largely on their own. This isn't a proof-of-concept demo; it's a real-world attack that already happened.

**Why it matters:** Autonomous AI-driven exploitation means the gap between vulnerability disclosure and active exploitation could collapse to near-zero, fundamentally breaking how defenders have always budgeted their response time.

**Source:** [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/amazon-says-hackers-ai-agents-found-and-exploited-0-day-with-little-human-input/)

## Jensen Huang Is Back at Nvidia and Already Playing Defense

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang held his first all-hands meeting since returning to the company, reportedly apologizing to staff and pushing back hard on reports that Trump and Silicon Valley power brokers orchestrated his rehiring. Huang called those claims "not true," though the optics of his return — amid intense White House cozying with tech executives — have made the denial a tough sell. The meeting was quickly leaked to Bloomberg, NYT, and The Handbasket.

**Why it matters:** Huang's return to leading the world's most strategically important AI chip company under a cloud of political entanglement is exactly the kind of governance drama that makes investors and foreign partners nervous.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041571/jensen-huang-all-hands-nvidia-apology)

## Huawei Convicted of Stealing Trade Secrets from T-Mobile, Cisco, and Motorola

A federal jury in New York found Huawei guilty of stealing trade secrets from multiple US companies, capping a legal battle that started with a DOJ criminal indictment in 2018. The stolen goods included source code and proprietary technology — the kind of IP that fuels next-generation networking and, increasingly, AI infrastructure. This is a criminal conviction, not a civil settlement.

**Why it matters:** The verdict hands US regulators and trading partners fresh legal ammunition to justify ongoing restrictions on Huawei's access to American chips, cloud services, and global telecom markets.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041820/huawei-guilty-trade-secrets-trial)

## Firefox 161 Ships With Per-Site VPN Rules and a One-Click History Nuke

Mozilla pushed Firefox 161 on Tuesday, adding granular VPN control that lets users set always-on or always-off rules per website — no more toggling manually. The update also includes a "Fire" button that wipes browsing history in a single click, a privacy UX feature that's been conspicuously absent from most mainstream browsers.

**Why it matters:** Firefox is quietly differentiating itself from Chrome on privacy tooling at a moment when browser market dynamics are finally showing some movement — these are the kinds of features that convert privacy-conscious power users.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1041474/firefox-161-vpn-rules-fire-button)

## FISA Section 702 Faces Last-Minute Congressional Scramble

With the legislative session nearly over, some lawmakers are trying to sneak a reauthorization of Section 702 — the warrantless wiretapping authority — into the must-pass National Defense Authorization Act. Sen. Mike Rounds floated the NDAA as a vehicle, which would let it bypass the kind of standalone debate the law's critics have been demanding. AI companies relying on government data-sharing programs have skin in this game too.

**Why it matters:** Section 702 sits at the intersection of surveillance law, tech company compliance obligations, and AI training data sourcing — letting it expire, or quietly reauthorizing it, both carry major downstream consequences for the industry.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/policy/1041568/fisa-ndaa-blue-wave)

## Take-Two Kills Fan-Built GTA VI PC Port Before It Could Get Started

A 15-year-old developer's project to translate a leaked GTA VI Xbox build to run natively on Windows was DMCA'd by Take-Two before it gained much traction. The project, called Leonida, was part of a broader community effort to get GTA VI on PC — a platform Rockstar has conspicuously ignored at launch. The leak it was based on came from a hacker who already faces legal consequences.

**Why it matters:** The aggressive takedown signals Take-Two's intent to control the PC release timeline and monetization window completely, with AI-assisted modding tools making these fan ports increasingly viable threats to publisher revenue.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/1041690/grand-theft-auto-6-pc-recompilation-project-leonida-takedown-take-two)
