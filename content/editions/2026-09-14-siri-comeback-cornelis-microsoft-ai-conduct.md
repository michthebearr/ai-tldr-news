## Siri Didn't Die — It Just Took Five Years to Get Good

Apple shipped iOS 27 and macOS 27 Golden Gate today, and the headline feature is a fully overhauled Siri AI that reviewers are actually recommending people use. The new Siri can answer real questions, handle cross-app tasks, and integrates deeply enough that third-party apps like fashion discovery tool Daydream can let users search products by camera roll photo — without opening the app. The update drops Intel Mac support entirely; Apple Silicon only from here.

**Why it matters:** Apple has 1.5 billion active iPhones — if even a fraction of those users start leaning on Siri daily, Apple's on-device AI play becomes one of the largest AI deployments on earth overnight.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/994756/apple-ios-27-launch-iphone-siri-ai-update)

## Cornelis Just Raised $205M to Fix the Part of AI Infrastructure Nobody Talks About

Cornelis Networks, which builds high-speed networking fabric that lets AI chips talk to each other faster, announced a $205 million funding round. The problem it's solving — chip interconnects — is increasingly the bottleneck as clusters scale beyond what Nvidia's own InfiniBand and NVLink can elegantly handle. More chips means more time wasted on chips waiting for each other.

**Why it matters:** Nvidia dominates the GPU market, but the interconnect layer is a real vulnerability — specialized networking startups that crack this could become critical infrastructure for every hyperscaler building out the next generation of training clusters.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/ai-infrastructure-company-cornelis-raises-205m-to-chip-away-at-nvidias-dominance/)

## Microsoft Published a "Code of Conduct" for Its AI Models — Yes, Really

Microsoft released a formal AI code of conduct that tells its models to not hack systems, not deceive users, and to support humans rather than replace them. It's a mix of high-minded principles ("accelerate human flourishing") and concrete safety guardrails baked into how the models are supposed to behave at a policy level.

**Why it matters:** A published code of conduct is only as strong as its enforcement mechanism, but it does create a paper trail that regulators, enterprise customers, and competitors can hold Microsoft to — which may be exactly the point.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/microsofts-new-ai-code-of-conduct-tells-models-not-to-hack-systems-or-trick-humans/)

## Superhuman Buys Meeting Notetaker Fathom to Build an Agentic Productivity Stack

Email startup Superhuman acquired Fathom, a YC-backed AI meeting notetaker with 400,000 monthly active users. The deal is a direct bet that the next battleground for productivity software isn't features — it's owning the full loop of communication: email, meetings, and whatever autonomous tasks come next.

**Why it matters:** Standalone AI notetakers are already being commoditized by Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams; Fathom's best shot at survival was getting acquired by a product that could plug meeting context into a broader agentic workflow, and Superhuman needed exactly that data layer.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/superhuman-acquires-yc-backed-notetaker-fathom-as-productivity-platforms-push-for-agentic-work/)

## Waymo Now Operates in 15 Cities After Las Vegas Launch

Waymo opened commercial robotaxi operations in Las Vegas, making it the company's 15th market. Vegas is a strategically interesting choice — high tourist volume, grid-style streets, and a city government that's been aggressively courting autonomous vehicle operators for years.

**Why it matters:** Waymo's methodical city-by-city expansion is quietly building the operational data and regulatory playbook that any competitor trying to catch up will need years to replicate.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/14/waymo-opens-robotaxi-service-in-las-vegas/)

## Jensen Huang Took Trump's Call Live Onstage — Then Talked Robots

Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang put President Trump on speakerphone during the All-In Summit, in what has become a running motif of the Huang era. Beyond the theater, Huang used his time onstage to argue that robots won't take over the world — a message that conveniently aligns with Nvidia's pitch that more AI infrastructure spending is good, actually.

**Why it matters:** When the most important chip CEO in the world is taking presidential calls live and opining on robot risk, it's a sign of how thoroughly AI infrastructure has become a geopolitical and political asset, not just a tech story.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/995079/president-donald-trump-calls-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-all-in-summit)
