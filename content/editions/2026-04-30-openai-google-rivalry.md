## OpenAI vs. Google: The Product War Is On

This week made it impossible to ignore: OpenAI and Google are in a full-scale product war. OpenAI shipped deep research integrations into ChatGPT, making it a near-replacement for Google Search for complex queries. Google fired back by announcing Gemini will be built into every Google Workspace product by Q3, with AI agents that can autonomously manage your Gmail, Calendar, and Docs.

**Why it matters:** The winner of this fight owns where people spend their work hours. Google has the distribution advantage; OpenAI has the mindshare. This battle will play out over the next 18 months and reshape enterprise software.

## Anthropic Launches Persistent Memory for Claude

Anthropic shipped a major update to Claude.ai: persistent memory that survives across conversations. Claude can now remember your preferences, ongoing projects, and communication style. There's a memory dashboard where you can see and edit what Claude knows about you. Enterprise customers get team-level shared memory.

**Why it matters:** Memory is the missing piece that makes AI assistants actually useful over time. This is Anthropic's answer to the "it forgets everything" complaint and directly competes with ChatGPT's existing memory feature.

## NVIDIA Announces Blackwell Ultra: 20x AI Performance

At GTC Spring, NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang unveiled Blackwell Ultra, the successor to the current Blackwell architecture. The new chip delivers 20x the AI inference performance of H100, with a focus on long-context and multi-modal workloads. First cloud availability expected in Q4 2026 via AWS, Azure, and GCP.

**Why it matters:** Model inference costs are the biggest bottleneck to AI going everywhere. 20x more efficient chips mean costs could drop dramatically by end of year — which unlocks use cases that are currently too expensive to run at scale.

## Runway Acquires ElevenLabs: Audio Meets Video

In a surprise acquisition, Runway — the AI video startup — acquired ElevenLabs, the leading AI voice platform, for a reported $1.2B. The combined company will offer a full audio-visual AI creation suite, with integration expected by end of year.

**Why it matters:** This is the most significant consolidation in the generative media space yet. A unified video + voice platform could accelerate AI-generated content from prototype to production quality for film, advertising, and gaming.
