## Corporate America Has Spent Billions on AI and Has Almost Nothing to Show For It

An Apollo Global economist is making a blunt case that AI has delivered "zero financial payoff" to most companies outside of the tech sector itself. Despite massive capital expenditures on infrastructure, software, and headcount, the productivity gains just aren't showing up in the financial data for the average Fortune 500 firm. The benefits, so far, are accruing almost entirely to the companies selling the picks and shovels.

**Why it matters:** This is the clearest high-profile articulation yet of the gap between AI hype and measurable business value — and it's coming from a major financial institution, not a skeptic blogger.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/25/ai-delivers-zero-financial-payoff-to-most-companies-according-to-apollo-economist/)

## Google's AI Search Overhaul Is Quietly Killing Online Publishing

The Verge's deep-dive this week lays out how Google's AI-generated search summaries have systematically drained traffic from independent publishers and media outlets. Sites that spent years and significant money on SEO optimization now find themselves buried under AI answers that summarize their work without sending readers anywhere. The shift has accelerated a crisis that was already battering digital media.

**Why it matters:** If Google's AI search becomes the destination rather than the directory, the economic model that funds most online journalism and content creation collapses entirely.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/column/1017983/google-ai-search-online-publishing)

## Reddit's r/opensource Just Banned All AI Projects — and the Internet Is Fighting About It

The moderators of r/opensource dropped a blunt new rule over the weekend: if your project uses AI, is built with AI, or is intended for AI, it's not welcome. The post explicitly states that "AI, as it exists today, is incongruent to this Open Source community." The announcement triggered a massive argument in the comments and apparently spurred calls to oust the mods entirely.

**Why it matters:** It's a flashpoint for a genuine ideological split in the developer community over whether AI-assisted code is compatible with open source values — and how that question gets answered will shape norms across the entire ecosystem.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018217/reddit-opensource-ai-ban)

## The AI Graveyard Is Getting Crowded: Siri, OpenAI's Super App, and More

TechCrunch is maintaining a running obituary list of AI projects that didn't survive — and it's a sobering read. Apple's Siri AI upgrades have been repeatedly delayed to the point of becoming a punchline, while OpenAI's attempt to launch a "super app" was, by most accounts, a mess. The list keeps growing as the gap between announced ambitions and shipped products widens.

**Why it matters:** The graveyard is a useful reality check against the constant drumbeat of AI announcements — shipping working products at scale remains genuinely hard, even for the best-funded players.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/the-ai-graveyard-a-running-list-of-projects-and-startups-that-didnt-make-it/)

## AI Is About to Expose How Fragile the Internet's Infrastructure Actually Is

A TechCrunch piece makes the uncomfortable argument that AI's voracious appetite for compute, bandwidth, and real-time data is going to stress-test infrastructure that was never built to handle it. The modern internet, as the piece puts it, is "held together by glue and dreams" — and AI workloads are about to find every crack. Legacy systems, aging data centers, and brittle APIs are all in the crosshairs.

**Why it matters:** The AI scaling story almost always focuses on models and chips, but the unsexy layer of internet infrastructure may be the actual bottleneck nobody is talking about enough.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/24/will-ai-expose-the-rickety-infrastructure-of-the-internet/)

## Blue Planet III Is Deliberately Keeping the Ugly Shots to Prove It's Real

BBC's Blue Planet III is taking an unusual editorial stance: leave in the imperfect, blurry, and rough footage on purpose. The reasoning is direct — AI-generated visuals tend to look impossibly polished, so genuine imperfection has become a signal of authenticity. Executive producers are leaning into warts-and-all filmmaking as a credibility marker in an era when audiences are increasingly suspicious of what's real.

**Why it matters:** When "too perfect" becomes a red flag, it fundamentally changes the aesthetics of documentary filmmaking — and signals how deeply AI skepticism is now baked into audience expectations.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/1018205/blue-planet-iii-authentic-organic-ai)
