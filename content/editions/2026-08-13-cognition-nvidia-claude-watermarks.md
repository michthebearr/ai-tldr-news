## Cognition Is Already Back for More Money — $40B This Time

The AI coding startup raised $1B at a $26B valuation just a few months ago, and is reportedly already in talks to raise again at a $40B valuation. That's a 54% jump in valuation in under a year, for a company most people outside AI circles still can't describe in a sentence.

**Why it matters:** Either AI coding agents are genuinely worth this much, or we're deep in a bubble that makes 2021 look measured.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/ai-coding-startup-cognition-reportedly-already-in-talks-to-raise-at-40b-valuation/)

## Nvidia's $500B Plan Is Basically Insurance for Its Own GPUs

Nvidia wants to keep financiers lending for AI infrastructure buildouts — even as GPUs age and depreciate. The plan would essentially create a financial ecosystem that preserves the value of existing hardware by keeping demand artificially high through structured lending.

**Why it matters:** If it works, Nvidia doesn't just sell chips — it controls the economic conditions that make those chips worth buying.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/nvidias-new-500b-plan-is-risky-but-brilliant-especially-for-aging-gpus/)

## Claude Now Watermarks Its Output, and Cheaters Are Very Upset

Anthropic rolled out watermarking on Claude-generated content, and some users hit social media to complain — because it will expose them using AI at work or in school without disclosure. The backlash is less about privacy and more about losing plausible deniability.

**Why it matters:** Anthropic is drawing a line between legitimate AI use and covert AI use, and the anger proves the latter is way more common than anyone admits.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/some-claude-users-are-mad-that-anthropics-new-watermarks-will-catch-them-cheating-at-their-jobs-classes/)

## Amazon Is Training on Twitch Streamers' Content — Unless They Say No

Amazon quietly made AI training on Twitch content opt-out by default. When the Twitch CPO was asked why not opt-in, he answered honestly: "If this was opt-in, nobody would opt in. That's honestly the answer."

**Why it matters:** That candid admission is a perfect encapsulation of how the entire AI training data industry operates — extract first, apologize if caught.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/amazon-will-train-on-twitch-streamers-content-by-default-unless-they-opt-out/)

## Microsoft Kills Copilot Features Nobody Was Using

Microsoft is merging its consumer and business Copilot apps into one and axing AI-generated podcasts, Group Chats, Deep Research, and its "Mico" character. The product consolidation is essentially a quiet admission that the original Copilot launch threw too many things at the wall.

**Why it matters:** Microsoft's Copilot strategy is maturing from "ship everything" to "ship what works" — a necessary correction, but it raises questions about what's actually driving enterprise adoption.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/13/microsoft-kills-off-unsuccessful-ai-features-while-merging-its-separate-copilot-apps/)

## Hinton, Li, and Ng Walk Into a Conference and Agree on Open Source

At the Ai4 conference, three of the most respected names in AI — Geoffrey Hinton, Fei-Fei Li, and Andrew Ng — made a joint case for keeping AI open, even as safety concerns grow. The rare alignment between a safety hawk (Hinton) and an open-source advocate (Ng) is notable.

**Why it matters:** When the godfather of deep learning and the loudest open-source advocate in AI are on the same page, the "open vs. closed" debate may be shifting more than the headlines suggest.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/12/as-ai-safety-concerns-mount-three-pioneers-make-the-case-for-staying-open/)
