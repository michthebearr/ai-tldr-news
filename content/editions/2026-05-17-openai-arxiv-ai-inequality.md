## Greg Brockman Is Back — and Running OpenAI's Product Strategy

OpenAI co-founder Greg Brockman is stepping back into a central role, taking charge of product strategy at the company. The move comes alongside reports that OpenAI plans to merge ChatGPT and its coding product Codex into a single unified offering. Brockman had taken an extended leave of absence in 2024, so his return signals Sam Altman is leaning on original leadership as OpenAI navigates intensifying competition.

**Why it matters:** Product strategy is where OpenAI's billion-dollar bets get made — Brockman owning that function is one of the more consequential internal moves the company has made in years.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/openai-co-founder-greg-brockman-reportedly-takes-charge-of-product-strategy/)

## arXiv Will Ban You for a Year If AI Writes Your Whole Paper

The preprint research repository arXiv is rolling out stricter enforcement against LLM-generated content, threatening one-year submission bans for authors who let AI do all the writing. The policy targets "careless" use — wholesale generation of papers — rather than AI-assisted editing or grammar fixes. It puts arXiv among the most aggressive academic gatekeepers when it comes to AI content.

**Why it matters:** With scientific publishing increasingly flooded by low-quality AI-generated papers, arXiv's hard line could pressure other journals and repositories to follow suit before the integrity of the research record degrades further.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/research-repository-arxiv-will-ban-authors-for-a-year-if-they-let-ai-do-all-the-work/)

## The AI Gold Rush Has a Wealth Gap Problem

TechCrunch's latest analysis paints a grim picture of who's actually winning the AI boom: a thin layer of hyperscalers, GPU owners, and well-funded startups, while the broader tech workforce and smaller companies are getting squeezed. Even inside the industry, the vibes are souring — layoffs continue while AI capex soars into the hundreds of billions. The gap between those building AI infrastructure and those displaced by it keeps widening.

**Why it matters:** The narrative that AI lifts all boats is cracking; how the industry handles — or ignores — this inequality will shape the political and regulatory environment for years.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/the-haves-and-have-nots-of-the-ai-gold-rush/)

## Cerebras Nearly Burned to Zero Before Becoming a $60B IPO

A new deep-dive reveals that Cerebras Systems — 2026's biggest tech IPO at a $60 billion valuation — was hemorrhaging $8 million a month in its early years and came within a hair of shutting down entirely. The company spent years and hundreds of millions building a chip that most of the industry thought was physically impossible: a wafer-scale AI processor the size of a dinner plate. The bet eventually paid off spectacularly.

**Why it matters:** Cerebras is a case study in what it takes to compete with Nvidia — obscene capital burn, extreme technical risk, and years of near-death experiences before the market caught up.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/60b-ai-chip-darling-cerebras-almost-died-early-on-burning-8m-a-month/)

## AI Chatbots at the Drive-Thru Are Becoming the New Normal

The Verge traces the arc of AI-powered drive-thru ordering from McDonald's 2021 experiments to today's widespread fast-food deployments at Wendy's and beyond. Early pilots were rocky — mishaps went viral — but the tech has matured, and chains are pressing forward anyway driven by labor cost pressure. The drive-thru is becoming a proving ground for AI in high-volume, low-margin consumer interactions.

**Why it matters:** Fast food is one of the largest low-wage employment sectors in the U.S.; the normalization of AI ordering at scale is a leading indicator of how quickly automation will move into physical retail jobs.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/column/928096/chatbots-ai-drive-thru-mcdonalds-wendys)

## Menlo Ventures Bets $30M on an AI-Native Marketing OS

Nectar Social, an AI-powered marketing platform, closed a $30 million Series A led by Menlo Ventures' Anthology Fund — the vehicle Menlo created in partnership with Anthropic. The startup is pitching itself as a full "marketing operating system" rather than a point tool. The Anthropic connection suggests Claude is likely powering or deeply integrated into its stack.

**Why it matters:** Menlo doubling down here via its Anthropic-tied fund signals a deliberate strategy to build an ecosystem of Claude-powered SaaS companies — a playbook straight out of the OpenAI/Microsoft partnership handbook.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/16/marketing-operating-system-nectar-social-raises-30m-series-a-in-round-led-by-menlo/)
