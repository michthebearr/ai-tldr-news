## Anthropic Writes a $1.5B Check — and the Copyright War Rages On

A federal judge approved Anthropic's $1.5 billion class action settlement with authors who accused the company of training Claude on pirated books. Each author gets roughly $3,000 per book — meaningful, but not exactly life-changing. The settlement closes this particular case but sets zero binding precedent on whether training AI on copyrighted material is legal in the first place.

**Why it matters:** Every major AI lab is watching this number. $1.5B is the new floor for what large-scale copyright litigation costs, and it won't be the last settlement we see.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/968724/anthropic-authors-settlement-ai-copyright-approved)

## Google Drops Three Gemini Models — Just Not the One Anyone Wanted

Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash, 3.5 Flash-Lite, and a new security-focused Flash Cyber model. All three are speed-and-cost optimized workhorses aimed at enterprise agent deployments. Still no Gemini 3.5 Pro — the flagship model the industry has been waiting on — which is starting to look less like a delay and more like a strategic decision nobody wants to explain publicly.

**Why it matters:** Google is flooding the zone with cheap inference models while OpenAI and Anthropic dominate the premium tier — a bet that volume and cost wins the enterprise agentic market.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/google-releases-three-new-gemini-models-but-no-3-5-pro/)

## Washington Floats Sanctions on Chinese AI Models Over IP Theft

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. could sanction Chinese open-weight AI models, citing alleged intellectual property theft. The timing is no accident — Moonshot AI's Kimi K3, the largest open-weight model yet released, dropped last week and immediately reignited the policy debate. Enterprises currently evaluating Chinese open-weight models now have a genuine legal risk hanging over those decisions.

**Why it matters:** If sanctions land, they'd be the most aggressive U.S. move yet to weaponize trade policy against Chinese AI — and would force enterprises to pick a side fast.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/us-threatens-sanctions-against-chinese-ai-models-over-ip-theft/)

## More Than Half of Deezer's Daily Uploads Are Now AI-Generated

Deezer disclosed that over 90,000 AI-generated tracks hit the platform every single day in June — more than 50% of total daily uploads. The streaming service is essentially drowning in synthetic music, and it has no clean way to separate it from human-made content at scale. This isn't a future problem; it's the present reality of what generative audio has done to music distribution.

**Why it matters:** When AI content outpaces human content on a major platform, the entire royalty, discovery, and monetization model breaks — and no one has a credible fix yet.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/music-streamer-deezer-says-more-than-50-of-daily-uploads-are-ai-generated/)

## Trump's AI Czar Seat Is a Revolving Door Nobody Wants to Sit In

The director of the Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) has resigned — again. Since David Sacks departed his AI czar role, the position has cycled through leadership at a pace that signals either total dysfunction or an intentional power vacuum at the top of U.S. AI governance. There's no indication a stable replacement is waiting in the wings.

**Why it matters:** While China executes a coordinated national AI strategy, the U.S. government can't keep an AI policy director in their chair long enough to unpack their desk.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/trumps-latest-ai-czar-has-already-resigned/)

## Suno Data Breach Hits 55 Million Users

A hacker accessed names, phone numbers, and physical addresses of 55 million users of AI music generator Suno, per data breach tracker Have I Been Pwned. Physical addresses being in the dataset makes this worse than a typical credential dump — it's the kind of data that enables real-world targeting, not just phishing.

**Why it matters:** As AI consumer apps scale to tens of millions of users, they become high-value breach targets — and most are moving too fast on growth to match it with security infrastructure.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/21/ai-music-generator-suno-breach-affects-55m-users-per-have-i-been-pwned/)
