## Trump Wants to Rebrand AI, Create an "AI Force," and Appoint an AI Czar

Trump posted on Truth Social that he plans to create a new "AI Force" led by an "AI czar," framing any pushback against AI development as a Democratic hoax. His administration, he says, will "cherish" the AI industry rather than regulate it — which tracks with his earlier executive order scrapping Biden-era AI safety guidelines. He also floated rebranding "AI" entirely, though he didn't offer an alternative name.

**Why it matters:** The creation of a dedicated AI Force signals that AI policy is now a full-blown political battleground, with the White House positioning itself as the industry's champion heading into a pivotal period for federal AI governance.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997867/trump-ai-force-ai-czar)

## Meta's Muse Is Useful, Unsettling, and Can't Explain What It Is

Meta's Muse AI assistant is drawing attention for its new Mac app, which can tap into your Messages, Calendar, and Notes. Users report it's genuinely capable — but also that it struggles to describe itself when asked directly, deflecting in ways that feel evasive rather than humble. One screenshot making the rounds shows Muse asking a user clarifying questions about its own nature.

**Why it matters:** An AI assistant with deep access to personal data that can't coherently explain what it is or what it does is a product design red flag and a privacy governance problem rolled into one.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/997833/meta-muse-creepy)

## A $249 Smart Ring Wants to Be Your AI Meeting Notes Taker

Vocci is pitching a lightweight wearable ring at TechCrunch Disrupt that passively listens during meetings and generates AI-powered notes. At $249, it's priced as a productivity tool, but the always-on audio capture immediately raises questions about consent from others in the room. It's the latest entrant in the crowded ambient AI wearable space.

**Why it matters:** As AI note-taking hardware moves from pendants and glasses to rings, the form factor war is heating up — but so are the unresolved questions around recording consent in professional settings.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/20/voccis-ring-adds-a-new-form-factor-to-meeting-note-taking/)

## Humans Are Still the Biggest Cybersecurity Threat to Energy Grids — AI Just Makes It Worse

A new report highlights that critical energy infrastructure was already dangerously exposed to cyberattacks long before AI entered the picture — and AI is now amplifying attacker capabilities faster than defenders can adapt. Security experts warn that the "rogue AI" narrative distracts from the much more immediate threat: human actors using AI tools to probe and exploit grid vulnerabilities at scale.

**Why it matters:** Policymakers fixated on sci-fi AI risks may be neglecting the unglamorous but urgent work of hardening the infrastructure that AI-powered attackers are actively targeting right now.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/science/997834/ai-cyberattack-energy-critical-infrastructure)

## ScrollEd Wants to Turn Your Textbook Into an Infinite Scroll Feed

Palo Alto startup ScrollEd, founded by student couple Utsav Gupta and Rebecca Neff, is pitching at TechCrunch Disrupt with a product that converts traditional textbooks into a TikTok-style scrollable feed complete with video, audio, and embedded quizzes. The bet is that if students already live in the scroll, education should meet them there. The AI handles the content reformatting automatically.

**Why it matters:** If it works at scale, ScrollEd could pressure traditional edtech players — and publishers — by making AI-driven content transformation the default expectation for learning materials.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/20/scrolled-wants-to-turn-textbooks-into-tiktok/)

## Flock Turns to Employee Buyouts to Avoid Layoffs

Surveillance camera and fleet management company Flock Safety is reportedly offering voluntary buyouts to employees as a way to trim headcount without formal layoffs. According to reports, without sufficient uptake on buyouts, the company would "almost certainly" need to move to cuts. It's a softer landing strategy, but one that signals real financial pressure.

**Why it matters:** Flock raised heavily during the AI/safety tech boom — its struggles are a canary for other well-funded but not-yet-profitable AI infrastructure companies facing the same reckoning.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/19/flock-reportedly-tries-to-shrink-workforce-with-employee-buyouts/)
