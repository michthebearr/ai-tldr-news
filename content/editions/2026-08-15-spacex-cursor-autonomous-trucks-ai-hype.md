## SpaceX Just Became an AI Coding Company

The Cursor acquisition is officially closed — Elon Musk's rocket company now owns one of the most widely used AI coding assistants on the market. This is a sharp left turn for SpaceX, which has historically stayed in its aerospace lane while sister company xAI handles Musk's AI ambitions.

**Why it matters:** Whether this is a talent grab, an internal tooling play, or something bigger, SpaceX owning a mainstream developer tool is a genuinely weird power move that nobody saw coming.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/15/spacex-officially-closes-its-cursor-acquisition/)

## Self-Driving Trucks Are Now Legally Testing on California Highways

Aurora Innovation and Kodiak AI have both received permits from the California DMV to run autonomous trucks on public highways. This marks a meaningful regulatory milestone — California has historically been one of the harder states to get autonomous vehicle permits from.

**Why it matters:** Trucking is a $800B+ industry, and two serious players getting California approval in the same week signals the regulatory tide is turning for commercial autonomous freight.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/self-driving-trucks-are-officially-testing-on-california-highways/)

## A Top VC Just Called Out Silicon Valley's AI Delusion

Thrive Capital's Joshua Kushner published his first-ever investor letter, and the headline message is a warning to his own industry: AI is a massive opportunity, but "it would be a grave error to let excitement weaken investment discipline." He's not bearish on AI — he's bearish on the valuations and deal terms that AI euphoria is producing.

**Why it matters:** When a top-tier growth investor who backed OpenAI tells the Valley to pump the brakes, it's worth taking seriously — especially as AI round sizes and valuations continue to defy gravity.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/thrives-joshua-kushner-chides-silicon-valley-vcs-over-ai-euphoria/)

## A Mac Screen-Sharing Bug Is Being Actively Exploited Right Now

A critical macOS vulnerability in the screen-sharing feature lets remote attackers log in without a password and gain full control of the machine. It's not theoretical — it's under active exploitation in the wild.

**Why it matters:** If you have screen sharing enabled on your Mac, you need to patch or disable it immediately — this is as bad as remote code execution vulnerabilities get.

**Source:** [Ars Technica](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/08/vulnerability-giving-attackers-full-control-of-macs-is-under-active-exploitation/)

## Iranian Hackers Are Breaking Into US Water Plants

Over the past few weeks, hackers allegedly linked to the Iranian government have successfully breached multiple US water utility systems. The full scope of access gained and any potential for operational disruption is still being assessed.

**Why it matters:** Critical infrastructure attacks aren't hypothetical anymore — water systems are soft targets, and this wave of intrusions is a preview of what state-sponsored cyberwar looks like in practice.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/14/what-we-know-about-the-alleged-iranian-hacks-on-u-s-water-utilities/)

## Your AI Platform Accounts Are a Hacking Target — Here's How to Check

TechCrunch put out a practical guide on detecting unauthorized access to accounts on major AI platforms including ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, and others. As these platforms store sensitive conversations, prompts, and increasingly agentic workflows, they've become high-value targets.

**Why it matters:** People treat AI chat accounts like throwaway logins, but they often contain sensitive business context, personal data, and access to connected tools — making a compromised account far more dangerous than it looks.
