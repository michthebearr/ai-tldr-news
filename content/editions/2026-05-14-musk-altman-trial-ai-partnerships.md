## Musk vs. Altman Trial Ends With Courtroom Drama

The biggest tech trial of the year concluded with closing arguments today, and Musk's legal team completely imploded. His lawyer Steven Molo stumbled over words, called co-defendant Greg Brockman "Greg Altman," and had to be corrected by the judge when he claimed Musk wasn't asking for money. Meanwhile, OpenAI employees gifted a researcher a trophy reading "Never stop being a jackass" — which became evidence.

**Why it matters:** This courtroom disaster could hand OpenAI a decisive victory in a case that will set precedents for AI company governance and founder disputes.

## OpenAI Preps Legal War Against Apple Over Failed Partnership

OpenAI is exploring legal action against Apple after their ChatGPT integration completely failed to deliver the subscribers and prominence OpenAI expected. The partnership, which was supposed to be a major win for both companies, has left OpenAI feeling burned and looking for legal remedies. This wouldn't be the first time Apple has left a partner feeling shortchanged after a high-profile collaboration.

**Why it matters:** A legal battle between OpenAI and Apple would reshape the AI partnership landscape and could force more transparent deal terms across the industry.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/openai-is-reportedly-preparing-legal-action-against-apple-it-wouldnt-be-the-first-partner-to-feel-burned/)

## Cerebras Explodes 108% in First Major Tech IPO of 2026

AI chip maker Cerebras raised $5.5 billion and watched its stock rocket 108% on its first trading day, kicking off what could be a massive year for tech IPOs. Just a year ago, it looked like this moment would never come for the company that makes massive AI training chips. The pop signals huge investor appetite for AI infrastructure plays.

**Why it matters:** Cerebras' success opens the floodgates for other AI companies to go public and proves the market's massive appetite for AI infrastructure investments.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)

## Elon's SpaceXAI Hemorrhages Staff After Merger

More than 50 employees have fled Elon Musk's newly merged SpaceXAI since February, raising serious questions about the company's direction. The exodus points to burnout from the merger chaos, leadership changes, talent poaching by competitors, and employees cashing out after liquidity events weakened their retention incentives. The bleeding talent could derail Musk's ambitious AI plans.

**Why it matters:** Losing this much talent this quickly suggests SpaceXAI's merger strategy is backfiring and could cripple its ability to compete with OpenAI and Anthropic.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/elon-musks-spacexai-has-been-bleeding-staff-since-its-merger/)

## Richard Socher Wants AI That Builds Itself

Former Salesforce chief scientist Richard Socher just raised $650 million to build an AI system that can research and improve itself indefinitely. His new startup promises to actually ship products, not just research papers, as it tackles the holy grail of artificial general intelligence. Socher insists this isn't just another AI lab burning through cash on theoretical breakthroughs.

**Why it matters:** Self-improving AI represents the ultimate goal of AGI research, and Socher's track record suggests this could be the team to actually pull it off.

## Microsoft Cancels Claude Code as OpenAI Fights Back

Microsoft is canceling Claude Code licenses for thousands of employees just months after rolling out Anthropic's popular AI coding tool. The move comes as OpenAI rushes to catch up by bringing Codex to mobile phones and cutting "side quests" like Sora to focus on developer tools. Microsoft's pullback suggests growing pressure to use homegrown AI tools over competitors.

**Why it matters:** The coding AI wars are heating up fast, and platform owners like Microsoft are starting to play favorites with their own AI investments.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/tech/930447/microsoft-claude-code-discontinued-notepad)
