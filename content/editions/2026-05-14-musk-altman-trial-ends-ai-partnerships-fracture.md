## Musk vs. Altman Trial Ends in Spectacular Legal Meltdown

The year's biggest tech trial concluded today with Elon Musk's lawyer stumbling through closing arguments, calling co-defendant Greg Brockman "Greg Altman" and erroneously claiming Musk wasn't seeking money. The jury will now decide whether OpenAI breached its founding agreement to remain a nonprofit focused on AI safety rather than profits.

**Why it matters:** This case could set precedent for how AI companies balance profit motives against their stated mission to benefit humanity.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/931006/musk-v-altman-closing-arguments-analysis)

## OpenAI Threatens Legal Action Against Apple Over Failed Partnership

OpenAI is exploring legal action against Apple after their ChatGPT integration failed to deliver the subscriber growth and prominence the AI company expected. The partnership was supposed to bring ChatGPT features to iOS users, but OpenAI feels Apple didn't hold up its end of the deal.

**Why it matters:** Big Tech partnerships are fracturing as AI companies realize they may need direct user relationships rather than relying on platform gatekeepers.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/openai-is-reportedly-preparing-legal-action-against-apple-it-wouldnt-be-the-first-partner-to-feel-burned/)

## SpaceXAI Hemorrhages Talent After Musk's Controversial Merger

More than 50 employees have left Elon Musk's SpaceXAI since the February merger of SpaceX and xAI, raising serious questions about leadership stability and employee retention. The exodus comes amid reports of burnout, management shake-ups, and weakened stock incentives following liquidity events.

**Why it matters:** Musk's ambitious plan to combine rocket science with AI development may be falling apart before it gets off the ground.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/elon-musks-spacexai-has-been-bleeding-staff-since-its-merger/)

## Cerebras Scores Massive $5.5B IPO, Stock Soars 108%

AI chip maker Cerebras pulled off 2026's first major tech IPO, raising $5.5 billion with shares jumping 108% on day one. The company, which makes massive AI training chips, had been struggling to go public just a year ago but rode the AI wave to a triumphant market debut.

**Why it matters:** The successful IPO signals renewed investor appetite for AI hardware companies and could open the floodgates for more tech listings.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/cerebras-raises-5-5b-kicking-off-2026s-ipo-season-with-a-bang/)

## Richard Socher Raises $650M to Build Self-Improving AI

Former Salesforce AI chief Richard Socher's new startup has raised $650 million to build AI systems that can research and improve themselves indefinitely. Unlike other research labs focused on theoretical breakthroughs, Socher promises his company will actually ship products to customers.

**Why it matters:** Self-improving AI could dramatically accelerate development cycles, but also raises obvious concerns about systems that evolve beyond human control.

**Source:** [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/14/what-happens-when-ai-starts-building-itself/)

## OpenAI Brings Codex to Mobile, Chasing Anthropic's Success

OpenAI is rolling out Codex, its desktop AI coding tool, to ChatGPT mobile apps as it races to catch up with Anthropic's popular Claude Code. The move comes as OpenAI has reportedly cut "side quests" like Sora video generation to focus on core products that can compete directly with Claude.

**Why it matters:** The AI coding wars are heating up as companies realize developers are willing to pay premium prices for tools that actually boost productivity.

**Source:** [The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/930763/openai-codex-chatgpt-ios-android-app-preview)
